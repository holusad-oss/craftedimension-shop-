FDM and resin

FDM (filament)

A hot nozzle melts plastic filament and draws the object in layers. It suits larger, practical pieces: stands, clips, planters, lithophanes.

There are visible layer lines on FDM. A sanded or painted finish softens them; a raw print shows them. FDM parts are usually tougher in everyday knocks than a small resin print.

Resin

A liquid resin is cured by UV light, often at about 0.05 mm, so miniatures and fine texture come out sharper than FDM.

In short, resin is brittle, not for outdoor use. A thin sword or a dish rim can snap if it is dropped or left in the sun. It is a poor choice for a part that must flex or live outside.

What each one is good for

PLA

Good for: display pieces, lithophanes, planters (ornamental only) and models that do not have to survive a hot room.

Strength: stiff. It holds its shape, then snaps rather than bending.

Heat: PLA softens in heat or a hot car. A dashboard, a radiator or a sunny windowsill can warp it. Keep it under about 50°C.

UV: long sun can fade the colour and make it more brittle.

Care: dust or wipe with a damp cloth. Not food-safe. No dishwasher, no long soak.

PETG

Good for: clips, brackets and other small working parts that get handled.

Strength: tougher than PLA and a little more giving, so a clip is less likely to snap.

Heat: copes with a warmer room better than PLA. It can still distort on a radiator or in a closed car on a hot day.

UV: holds up better than PLA, but it is still not an outdoor material for months in the sun.

Care: wipe clean. Not food-safe. It can scratch more easily than PLA.

Standard resin

Good for: ornaments, dishes for jewellery, and anything where the surface detail matters more than toughness.

Strength: hard, and brittle. Fine edges chip.

Heat and UV: heat can soften a thin part. UV yellows it and makes it more brittle. Not for outdoor use.

Care: dust it. Do not soak it, and do not leave it in a window. Not food-safe.

ABS-like resin

Good for: tabletop miniatures and small figures that will be handled, still unpainted or painted at home.

Strength: a bit less fragile than standard resin. It is still brittle next to PETG, and a thin weapon can snap.

Heat and UV: same warning as other resins: heat and sun age it. Not for outdoor use.

Care: prime before painting if you want paint to stay put. Not food-safe. Don't drop it onto a hard floor.

Tough resin

Good for: small functional parts that need finer detail than FDM and a bit more give than standard resin.

Strength: the least brittle of the resins here. It can still crack at a sharp corner, and it is not a substitute for a nylon or PETG clip that must flex every day.

Heat and UV: sunlight still ages it. Not for outdoor use, and not for a hot car.

Care: wipe, don't soak. Not food-safe.
